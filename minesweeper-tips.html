15 Minesweeper Tips to Win More Games

Practical habits that fix the mistakes losing you games — beginner to pro.

Most minesweeper losses come from a handful of repeatable mistakes: clicking on autopilot, flagging on a hunch, guessing too early. These fifteen tips, ordered roughly from beginner to advanced, target exactly those habits. For fundamentals, read the rules guide first; for depth, see the pattern and strategy guides.

Beginner Tips

1. Never click the corner first

Open near the center. A central first click cascades into more territory and produces richer numbers. Corner openings give you one or two clues and a slow, cramped game.

2. Treat the first click as free information

Your first reveal is always safe in Bean Boom. If the opening is poor (a single number surrounded by covered cells), immediately open a second area instead of forcing deductions.

3. A satisfied number means everything else is safe

If a number touches exactly that many flags, every other covered neighbor is safe. This is the most basic free-cell rule in the game — check it after every flag you place.

4. A number equal to its covered neighbors means all mines

If a "3" has exactly three covered cells around it, all three are mines. Flag them instantly. This pairs with tip 3 to unlock most beginner boards.

5. Slow down on Hard

Higher mine density punishes sloppy clicks. On Hard boards, verify each deduction against two neighboring numbers before you commit. Accuracy first, speed later.

Intermediate Tips

6. Learn the 1-2 pattern cold

The 1-2 along a wall guarantees a mine beyond the 2 and a safe cell beyond that. It appears in nearly every game; recognizing it instantly doubles your effective speed. See all core patterns here.

7. Flag only proven mines

A speculative flag is a landmine in your own logic: every later deduction inherits the error. If you are "pretty sure", don't flag — leave the cell alone and revisit when more numbers arrive.

8. Re-read the neighborhood after every reveal

Each new number changes what old numbers mean. Players who re-scan locally after every click find safe cells that impatient players miss entirely.

9. Use chording

Click a satisfied number to reveal all its remaining neighbors at once. Chording collapses five clicks into one and — just as important — removes the risk of mis-clicking one of those cells.

10. Clean the walls

Edges and corners concentrate the classic patterns. When the middle of the board stalls, sweep the perimeter: 1-1, 1-2, and 1-2-1 configurations along walls usually hand you a chain of free cells.

Advanced Tips

11. Reduce big numbers before reasoning

A "4" next to two flags is just a "2". Mentally subtract satisfied mines from every number before you analyze it — big-number clusters become small-number patterns you already know.

12. Defer, don't guess

Hit an ambiguous region? Mark it, move on, and keep extracting guaranteed cells elsewhere. Most "impossible" regions resolve once neighboring cells open up or the endgame counter arrives.

13. Count mines to close the game

When the mine counter is low, switch from local logic to global arithmetic: which remaining regions must contain mines, and therefore which regions must be entirely safe. Full method in the strategy guide.

14. Guess with information gain

When a guess is truly forced, pick a cell whose outcome teaches you the most — cells that satisfy multiple numbers at once. A 50/50 corner guess that also opens a new region beats a pure coin flip.

15. Play daily, track your win rate

Improvement is mechanical: play a few boards daily, and log wins per difficulty. Push your win rate toward 90% on Easy before grinding Hard times. Bean Boom's global leaderboard gives you a target to chase.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get better at minesweeper?

Three levers matter most: memorize the core patterns (1-1, 1-2, 1-2-1), re-read surrounding numbers after every flag, and count remaining mines in the endgame. Speed comes later — accuracy first.

What is a good minesweeper time?

On expert boards, clearing under two minutes is solid for casual players, under one minute is strong, and competitive players finish in well under a minute. Focus on win rate first, then times.

Should I guess in minesweeper?

Guess only when no forced conclusion exists anywhere on the board. Before guessing, sweep the whole board once more — most 'forced' guesses dissolve when a distant pattern or the mine counter is taken into account.
