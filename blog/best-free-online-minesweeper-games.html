7 Best Free Online Minesweeper Games (2026)

No downloads, no paywalls, no nonsense — just free minesweeper in your browser.

Minesweeper has been a browser staple since the late 1990s, but the landscape of free online minesweeper games has changed dramatically. Some are faithful recreations of the Windows classic. Others add scoring systems, chain reactions, or competitive leaderboards. A few are barely playable on mobile.

We tested seven of the most popular free online minesweeper games and compared them on the criteria that actually matter: mobile support, difficulty options, additional features beyond classic play, load speed, and whether you need to create an account. Here's what we found.

1. Bean Boom Our Pick

Bean Boom

URL: bb.superzan.net

Bean Boom is the only game on this list that offers two genuinely different modes. Classic Mode is pure traditional minesweeper — reveal all safe cells, avoid mines, race the clock. Egg Mode adds a scoring layer on top: correctly flagging a mine triggers a "Bean Boom" chain explosion that cascades across the board, and consecutive clears build a combo multiplier that can push your score into the thousands.

The game runs entirely in your browser with no download or signup. Touch controls are first-class — tap to reveal, long-press to flag, double-tap to chord. Global leaderboards are built in for both modes, and there's a paid challenge system where you can wager on clearing a board and get your money back if you succeed.

Best for: Players who want more than just classic minesweeper — combos, chain reactions, and a reason to keep playing beyond the timer.

2. Minesweeper Online

Minesweeper Online

A clean, no-frills recreation of classic minesweeper. The board looks exactly like the Windows XP version, which is either a selling point or a limitation depending on your taste. Three difficulty levels (Easy, Intermediate, Expert) match the Microsoft standard. The timer is prominent, and personal bests are saved locally.

Mobile support is the weak point: the game technically works on phones but the cell sizes are small and there's no long-press-to-flag gesture. Right-click flagging works perfectly on desktop.

Best for: Desktop players who want the authentic Windows look and nothing else.

3. Google Minesweeper

Google Minesweeper

Type "play minesweeper" into Google and this is what comes up. It's a polished, animated take on classic minesweeper with smooth reveal animations and full touch support. Three difficulty levels are available, and the first click is always safe.

The downside is that it's intentionally minimalist — no leaderboards, no scoring beyond the timer, no flagging strategy options. It's also not easily linkable (you access it through Google's built-in game widget), so you can't bookmark a specific game session.

Best for: Quick casual games when you just want to kill five minutes.

4. 247 Minesweeper

247 Minesweeper

Part of the 247 Games portal, this minesweeper offers multiple visual themes (classic, dark, festive) which is a nice touch. The gameplay is standard classic minesweeper with three difficulties.

The interface is ad-heavy, and mobile play is inconsistent — the page layout doesn't always scale well on smaller screens. There are no leaderboards or scoring innovations.

Best for: Players who like switching up the visual theme.

5. Minesweeper.io

Minesweeper.io

The standout feature here is multiplayer: you share a board with other players in real time, each racing to clear territory. It's a fun twist if you've exhausted solo play. The classic solo mode is also available.

However, the multiplayer mode requires an account, and the mobile experience is clunky — the real-time element makes precise tapping critical, and the touch targets are tight.

Best for: Social players who want to compete directly against others.

6. CardGames.io Minesweeper

CardGames.io Minesweeper

This is minesweeper as one game in a larger portal of classic card and board games. The implementation is clean and correct, with a slightly cartoonish visual style. Three difficulties are available.

There's nothing wrong with it, but there's nothing standout either — no leaderboards, no scoring features, no mobile optimization beyond basic responsiveness. It's a reliable backup if your usual site is down.

Best for: Players who also enjoy solitaire, hearts, or spades and want one site for everything.

7. Windows Classic (Emulator)

Windows Classic Emulator

Several sites host browser-based emulators of the original Windows 3.1 or Windows XP minesweeper. These are pixel-perfect recreations of the game you grew up with, complete with the gray beveled cells and the smiley face reset button.

The nostalgia is real, but so are the limitations: no mobile support at all, no modern features, and the emulated interface can feel sluggish compared to native web implementations.

Best for: A nostalgic trip down memory lane on a desktop.

How We Evaluated

We tested each game on both desktop (Chrome on Windows) and mobile (Safari on iPhone, Chrome on Android) across three criteria:

  1. Core gameplay — Does it implement minesweeper correctly? First-click safety, number accuracy, flood-fill behavior, chord support.
  2. Platform accessibility — Can you play it on any device without downloading anything? Are touch controls actually usable?
  3. Value beyond classic — Does it offer anything beyond bare-minimum classic minesweeper? Scoring, leaderboards, difficulty options, game modes.

Bean Boom scored highest because it's the only game that delivers both a faithful classic mode and a genuinely new mode (Egg Mode) that changes how you think about the board. What About Minesweeper Apps?

If you specifically want a downloadable app, the iOS App Store and Google Play both have dozens of free minesweeper apps. The trade-off is storage space, ads (often full-screen interstitials), and the fact that browser games have caught up in quality. Frequently Asked Questions

FAQ

Is Bean Boom really free? Yes. Both Classic Mode and Egg Mode are completely free to play in your browser. The optional paid challenges let you wager money, but they're entirely separate from the free game.

Do any of these games require a download? No. All seven games in this list run in a web browser. Some (like Minesweeper.io) offer optional app versions, but the browser version is fully functional.

Which game is best for beginners? Google Minesweeper and Bean Boom both offer forgiving Easy difficulty (9×9, 10 mines) with guaranteed-safe first clicks. Bean Boom's beginner guide walks you through your first game step by step.

Can I play minesweeper offline? Browser-based games require an internet connection to load, but some (including Bean Boom) cache well and can continue playing if your connection drops mid-game.
