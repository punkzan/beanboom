How to Play Minesweeper

A complete guide to the rules, strategies, and common patterns of minesweeper.

Minesweeper is a classic logic puzzle game where your goal is to reveal all safe cells on a grid without detonating any hidden mines. This guide covers everything you need to know to start playing Bean Boom — a free online minesweeper game with a unique bead art style.

The Basics

The game board is a grid of covered cells. Beneath some cells are mines (also called bombs). Your job is to reveal every cell that does not contain a mine.

  • Reveal: Click (desktop) or tap (mobile) a cell to uncover it.
  • Flag: Right-click (desktop) or long-press (mobile) a cell to mark it as a suspected mine.
  • Win: Reveal all safe cells without hitting a mine.
  • Lose: Reveal a cell with a mine underneath.

The first click is always safe — you will never hit a mine on your first reveal.

How to Read the Numbers

When you reveal a safe cell, it may show a number from 1 to 8. This number tells you exactly how many mines are hidden in the eight surrounding cells (including diagonals).

  • A 1 means exactly one mine is adjacent to that cell.
  • A 3 means three mines are nearby.
  • An empty cell (no number) means zero mines are adjacent — the game automatically reveals all connected empty cells.

By combining the numbers from multiple cells, you can deduce exactly where mines are and where it is safe to click.

Flagging Mines

Once you are confident a cell contains a mine, flag it so you don't accidentally click it later. Flagging is optional — you win by revealing all safe cells, not by flagging all mines — but it helps you keep track.

In Bean Boom, you can switch between Reveal mode and Flag mode using the buttons above the game board, or use right-click on desktop and long-press on mobile.

Common Minesweeper Patterns

Experienced players recognize recurring number patterns that make it easy to identify mines and safe cells:

The 1-1 Pattern

If two "1" cells are next to each other along a wall or edge, and one adjacent cell is already revealed, the mine must be under the unrevealed cell next to the "1" that has fewer unknown neighbors.

The 1-2 Pattern

If you see a "1" next to a "2" along a straight edge, the cell beyond the "2" is always a mine. The "1" is satisfied by the mine next to the "2", and the "2" needs one more mine further along.

The Corner Rule

A corner cell has only three neighbors. If a "1" is in the corner, the mine is among those three cells. Use adjacent numbers to narrow it down further.

Tips for Beginners

  1. Start from the center of the board — you will have more information from surrounding numbers.
  2. When stuck, look for cells where the number equals the count of unrevealed neighbors — all of them are mines.
  3. If a number equals the number of flags around it, all other adjacent cells are safe to reveal.
  4. Start with Easy difficulty (fewer mines) and work your way up to Hard.
  5. Don't guess unless absolutely necessary — most minesweeper boards can be solved with pure logic.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the first click always safe?

Yes. In Bean Boom, your first reveal will never hit a mine. The board is generated after your first click to ensure this.

What do the difficulty levels mean?

Easy has fewer mines and a smaller board, making it perfect for beginners. Medium adds more mines. Hard has the highest mine density for a real challenge.

Do I have to flag every mine to win?

No. You win by revealing all safe cells. Flagging is just a tool to help you remember where mines are.

Can I play minesweeper on my phone?

Yes. Bean Boom works on mobile browsers with touch controls — tap to reveal, long-press to flag.
